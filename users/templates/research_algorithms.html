Quantum algorithms & compilation

We develop practical quantum algorithms and compilation workflows for near-term devices. Our focus is on translating algorithmic ideas into implementable circuits with clear performance goals, while accounting for hardware constraints such as gate sets, connectivity, noise, and measurement cost.

Overview

Our workflow connects three layers: (i) algorithm design (what to compute), (ii) compilation (how to express it as circuits), and (iii) execution-aware optimization (how to run it reliably on real hardware).

Schematic of algorithm → circuit → compiled circuit → execution/feedback loop, aiming for reproducible and device-aware performance.

Core methods

We combine algorithm development with circuit-level optimization to make near-term executions more reliable. Typical ingredients include:

• Device-aware compilation (connectivity, gate sets, depth, error budget)
• Circuit synthesis for target unitaries (e.g., Toffoli and multi-qubit primitives)
• Gradient-based optimization and parameter-shift rules (PSR) with verification tools
• Execution-aware evaluation (cost landscape, noise sensitivity, reproducibility)

Research topics

1) Quantum compilation and circuit synthesis
We design workflows to compile abstract circuits into hardware-executable forms. This includes routing under limited connectivity, reducing depth and two-qubit gate counts, and synthesizing target unitaries with constrained gate sets. A key goal is to preserve algorithmic performance while respecting realistic device limitations.

2) QAOA for combinatorial optimization
We study QAOA for structured problems (e.g., scheduling, layout, selection, and graph-based objectives). Our emphasis is on practical performance: selecting mixers/cost constructions, improving trainability, and understanding when and why QAOA reaches good solutions under finite depth and noise.

3) VQE and variational ground-state preparation
We develop VQE-style strategies for quantum simulation and chemistry-inspired objectives, with attention to ansatz design, gradient reliability, and robust optimization under measurement constraints. We also compare variational approaches with alternative feedback/control-based schemes.

4) FALQON and feedback-based quantum control
We explore feedback-based algorithms such as FALQON, where parameter updates are driven by measurable quantities rather than classical training loops. We study when feedback helps (e.g., avoiding barren plateaus), how to improve robustness under degeneracies/noise, and how to hybridize feedback with imaginary-time or adaptive schedules.

Why it matters

Near-term quantum devices are limited by noise and resources. Practical progress requires algorithms that can be executed reliably and compilers that translate abstract circuits into hardware-friendly implementations. Our goal is to build algorithm + compilation pipelines that are transparent, reproducible, and useful for real experiments.
