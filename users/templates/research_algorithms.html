Quantum metrology and sensing

We develop time-dependent and networked quantum sensing protocols that enhance precision beyond classical limits. Our work connects quantum estimation theory, optimal control, and realistic noise models to design sensing strategies that are both theoretically grounded and experimentally relevant.

Core methods

Our approach combines tools from quantum estimation theory with practical optimization techniques. Typical ingredients include:

• Quantum Fisher information (QFI) and quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM)
• Time-dependent control for probe dynamics
• Noise modeling and robustness analysis
• Variational and learning-based optimization of states and measurements

Research topics

1) Time-dependent sensing protocols
Designing control schedules that maximize information gain within a fixed sensing time.

2) Networked quantum sensors
Distributed sensing using entangled or correlated nodes, optimized for scalability and noise.

3) Multiphase and multiparameter estimation
Understanding trade-offs, compatibility conditions, and fundamental limits in joint estimation.

4) Robust measurement design
Constructing POVMs that remain near-optimal under calibration errors and imperfect readout.

Why it matters

Precision measurement underpins modern science and technology. By designing protocols that explicitly account for noise, control constraints, and finite resources, our work aims to bridge the gap between fundamental limits and real-world quantum sensors.
