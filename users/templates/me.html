Research Vision

My research vision is to establish a unified framework that connects quantum estimation theory, control, and algorithms with realistic quantum hardware constraints. I aim to develop quantum protocols that are not only theoretically optimal but also practically viable under noise, finite data, and limited control.

  • Quantum metrology and sensing: Understand when quantum resources such as entanglement, squeezing, and nonlinearity genuinely improve multi-parameter precision.
  • Quantum algorithms and control: Design feedback-based and variational algorithms with stable optimization behavior and predictable performance.
  • Quantum energy technologies: Explore collective quantum effects to optimize charging speed, power, and robustness in quantum batteries.
  • Theory to platforms: Translate abstract concepts such as QFI, QFIM, and optimal control into protocols compatible with near-term quantum devices.

Ultimately, my goal is to deliver practical quantum advantages in sensing, computation, and energy applications by tightly integrating theory, numerical optimization, and experimental constraints.
