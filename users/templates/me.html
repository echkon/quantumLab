I lead Quantar Lab at Tohoku University, focusing on quantum algorithms, quantum metrology, quantum sensing, and quantum energy technologies. My work bridges foundational theory and practical protocols under realistic constraints such as noise, finite measurements, and limited control.

Email: binho@fris.tohoku.ac.jp

Research Vision

My research vision is to establish a unified framework that connects quantum estimation theory, control, and algorithms with realistic quantum hardware constraints. I aim to develop protocols that remain effective under noise, finite data, and limited control.

  • Quantum metrology & sensing: Identify when and how quantum resources (entanglement, squeezing, nonlinearity) genuinely improve multiparameter precision.
  • Quantum algorithms & control: Design feedback-based and variational methods with stable optimization behavior and predictable performance.
  • Quantum energy technologies: Explore collective quantum effects to optimize charging speed, power, and robustness in quantum batteries.
  • Theory to platforms: Translate concepts such as QFI/QFIM and optimal control into protocols compatible with near-term devices.

The long-term goal is to deliver practical quantum advantages in sensing, computation, and energy applications by integrating theory, numerical optimization, and experimental constraints.
